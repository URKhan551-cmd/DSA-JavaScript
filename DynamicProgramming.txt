How to approach an unfamiliar DP problem
This is the most important part. I want you to develop a repeatable problem-solving method.

Whenever you encounter a potential DP problem, follow these seven steps.
1. Understand the problem
   What is the input? What is the output? Are you counting possibilities, finding a minimum, maximizing something, or checking whether something is possible?
2. Identify the smaller subproblem
   Can the original problem be broken into smaller versions of itself?
3. Check overlapping subproblems
   Does solving one branch cause you to calculate the same smaller problem in another branch?
4. Define the state
   Decide what dp[i], dp[i][j], or another state represents in plain English.
5. Derive the transition
   Express how the current answer depends on previously solved states.
6. Identify base cases and evaluation order
   Determine where computation starts and which dependencies must be available first.
7. Analyze and optimize
   Calculate time and space complexity. Consider whether memoization, tabulation, or space optimization is appropriate.
One important distinction: not every problem involving recursion is a DP problem. If subproblems do not overlap, ordinary recursion may be sufficient. And if a greedy choice can be proven correct, DP may be unnecessary.

We won't jump straight into difficult 2D DP or advanced LeetCode problems. We'll build the mental models progressively.
